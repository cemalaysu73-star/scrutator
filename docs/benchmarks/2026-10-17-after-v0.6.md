# Scrutator after v0.6, against the same tools and targets: 2026-10-17

Same harness (`bench/run_bench.py`), same four frozen targets, same 900 s timeout, same competitors
as the baseline in `2026-10-17-baseline.md`. Scrutator code at commit `17d9142` (the version string
was bumped to 0.6.0 afterwards, so the raw results still say 0.1.0). Competitors were run again in the
same session. Raw outputs: `bench/results/2026-10-17-v06/` (run 1) and the variance table
`bench/results/2026-10-17-v06/variance.md` (runs 2 and 3 re-run Scrutator only).

## Verdict on the frozen criterion

Frozen criterion: recall at least the best competitor on every truth target, every result
evidence-backed, bundle verifies.

| Target | Truth | Scrutator recall | Best competitor | Evidence-backed | Bundle verifies (pinned) | Met |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| zonetransfer.me | 31 zone names (published AXFR dump) | 13% (4 of 31; 27% of the 15 host names) | knockpy 6% | 100% (6 of 6) | PASS | yes, but 87% of the zone is unreached by every tool |
| example.com | 1 live name | 100% | sublist3r and knockpy 100% | 100% (497 of 497) | PASS | yes (tie) |
| nmap.org | 4 live names | 100% (4 of 4) | knockpy 100% | 100% (347 of 347) | PASS | yes (tie) |
| owasp.org | 58 live names | 91% (runs 2 and 3: 87%) | knockpy 76% (runs 2 and 3: 81%) | 100% (132 of 132) | PASS | yes |

**On the numbers, the criterion is met on all four targets, in all three runs.** Read that with the
limits below before believing it: two of the four truth sets are one and four names, the other three
are circular by construction (the union of what the tools found, which Scrutator now supplies most
of), and the one real truth set shows Scrutator reaching 13% of it. "Evidence-backed" means every
reported name cites an evidence record that names it; it does not mean the name is real, which is what
the name states below are for. The baseline said not met; what changed is below.

## What differed from the baseline run (read this first)

- **HackerTarget's free quota (50 calls per day per IP) was used up** by my development runs from this
  IP. In this benchmark Scrutator's `hackertarget` module failed with "daily quota used up" on three of
  four targets (recorded, not hidden), and so did knockpy's `hackertarget` service: re-attributing
  owasp.org gave 0 names from it, where the baseline had 50 (49 live). So knockpy's live count on
  owasp.org fell from 56 to 44 for a reason that has nothing to do with the tools, and Scrutator is
  also without the same 50 names. Both numbers are understated. I could not re-measure after the
  quota reset.
- **crt.sh was down for most runs** (HTTP 502, once 404). Scrutator's `crtsh` failed on example.com,
  nmap.org and owasp.org in run 1. **CertSpotter answered all four in run 1.** Unauthenticated, it allows
  about ten calls, and it returned HTTP 429 in runs 2 and 3 (three targets each, plus one 60 s timeout in
  run 3). Both certificate sources were down together on nmap.org and owasp.org in runs 2 and 3 and on
  example.com in run 2; the reports were still written, and say so under Source coverage.
- **dnsrecon (crt) errored on example.com and nmap.org** (crt.sh 502; on example.com it then crashed on
  the empty answer). It is not a meaningful competitor in these runs.
- **Common Crawl never connected** (ConnectTimeout), as in the baseline; Wayback timed out on
  example.com in every run (60 s limit).
- Subdomain Center's free tier returns a **random sample of at most 500 names**, which is why owasp.org
  moved from 91% to 87% between runs.
- Not run, as before: subfinder, Amass, SpiderFoot, theHarvester. Nothing here says how Scrutator
  compares with them.

## Time

Time is still lost, and it is not in the frozen criterion. Scrutator took 52 to 123 s per target;
knockpy took 2 to 4 s, sublist3r 11 to 24 s. The baseline's 147 to 2133 s came down by a factor of 2 to
26 for the same targets, and a scan can no longer run past its deadline (default 600 s, tested with a
source that never answers). I profiled owasp.org during development (before the aggregators were added):
the DNS stage took about 90 s because the first system nameserver here is dead and each lookup waited
2 s; after asking the six record types together and preferring the server that answered, the same 45 DNS
runs took about 1 s.
This benchmark's runs were not profiled, so I do not claim where their 52 to 123 s went beyond that the
slowest source in the first round (crt.sh up to 90 s, Wayback up to 60 s, Common Crawl about 17 s of
connect timeouts) and the per-address lookups (RDAP, ASN, InternetDB, RIPEstat) are all in the path.

## What changed to get here

1. A global deadline, a per-source circuit breaker and root-only name sources: a scan always ends, a
   failing source is marked down and said so, and crt.sh, CertSpotter, Wayback, Common Crawl and the
   aggregators are asked once per scan about the apex, not once per name.
2. CertSpotter as a second certificate source: it answered every first-run target while crt.sh was down.
3. Subdomain Center, urlscan.io, HackerTarget and mnemonic passive DNS: Subdomain Center and urlscan are
   most of the new names; HackerTarget's quota was spent before the benchmark; mnemonic adds history,
   not subdomains.
4. Shodan InternetDB and RIPEstat contacts for in-scope addresses, with a low-confidence risky-port risk.
5. Name states with a recorded wildcard probe, re-derived by `verify`.

## Where Scrutator still loses, ranked

1. **Time.** 52 to 123 s against 2 to 4 s for knockpy. Rounds wait for the slowest source and every
   address gets four lookups. Next: a streaming scheduler that starts DNS for a name the moment a source
   names it, shorter timeouts for archives, and a `--fast` profile without the per-address enrichment.
2. **The zone's structure is out of reach of passive tools.** 27 of 31 names in zonetransfer.me (`vpn`,
   `owa`, `dc-office`, `asfdbbox`, `intns1`, the SRV and NAPTR owners) were found by nobody. That is where
   a scope-gated zone transfer or NSEC walk belongs (planned, active).
3. **Netcraft.** sublist3r's only working engine found 5 live names on owasp.org that nobody else did.
   Its terms were not evaluated, so it is not in Scrutator.
4. **Rate-limited sources fail on repeat runs.** HackerTarget's 50 per day was gone, and CertSpotter
   answered one run in three. A cross-scan record of each source's quota and backoff, or an optional key,
   would keep them useful; as it is they are best-effort and the report says when they were not available.
5. **Capped, random-order sources make results and replay vary.** Subdomain Center returns a random 500;
   owasp.org moved 91% to 87%, and replay reports unsampled names as changed.
6. **The report still counts claims as findings.** example.com has 556 findings for 1 live name. The name
   states are in the report, the findings table tags them and the counts say so, but stale names are not
   collapsed or sorted below live ones.
7. **Terms.** InternetDB, RIPEstat, CertSpotter and Subdomain Center are free for non-commercial use only,
   and the README says so. There is no switch yet to run without them for commercial work.
8. **The wildcard probe covers the apex only**, not wildcards below a delegated sub-zone.

## Reproduce

```
python bench/run_bench.py --date 2026-10-17-v06                      # about 8 minutes
python bench/run_bench.py --date X --reuse-from bench/results/2026-10-17-v06 --rerun scrutator   # variance runs
python bench/summarize_runs.py 2026-10-17-v06 X Y
python bench/attribute_sources.py --date 2026-10-17-v06              # per-source attribution on owasp.org
```

The public sources change and fail from run to run, and the HackerTarget quota is per IP address and
per day: expect different numbers.
