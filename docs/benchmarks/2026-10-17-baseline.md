# Scrutator against passive recon tools: baseline, 2026-10-17

Scrutator 0.1.0 at commit `6fcf461` (v0.5), run against the PyPI-installable passive tools, on four
frozen public targets, with the harness in `bench/` (`python bench/run_bench.py`). Raw outputs:
`bench/results/2026-10-17/`. Targets and the bar are those of the frozen criterion in [docs/benchmarks.md](../benchmarks.md).

Verdict against the frozen criterion (recall at least the best competitor on every truth target,
every result evidence-backed, bundle verifies): **not met.** Evidence-backed and verifiable: yes, on
every scan. Recall: ties on the control, ahead of every competitor on the one real truth set (but at
13%), and well behind the best competitor on owasp.org and nmap.org.

## Not apples to apples (read before the tables)

- **Truth for zonetransfer.me is a snapshot, not a live AXFR.** The single AXFR against
  `nsztm1.digi.ninja` timed out twice from this network (SOA over TCP/UDP answered), so truth is the
  dig AXFR dump the zone's author publishes on digi.ninja: 31 names below the apex, 15 with
  A/AAAA/CNAME. It is an old snapshot (SOA serial 2014101601; the live SOA says 2019100801).
- **Pseudo-truth is circular.** For the other targets, truth is the live names any tool found.
  It rewards the tool with the widest output (knockpy supplied most of it) and cannot reward a name
  nobody found. nmap.org has wildcard DNS (every label resolves to 50.116.1.184), so almost
  everything there is "wildcard-covered", existence unproven; its truth is 4 names and tells little.
  example.com's truth is one name.
- **Competitors are run narrowly on purpose.** dnsrecon only scrapes the Common Name column of the
  crt.sh HTML page (no SANs). knockpy's CLI would also AXFR and probe HTTP, so only its passive
  engine was run. sublist3r 1.0 predates the sources it targets: ThreatCrowd and PassiveDNS are
  dead and VirusTotal blocks it; only Netcraft produced names.
- **Sources are flaky and the runs differ.** crt.sh answered HTTP 502 in some runs and Common Crawl
  never connected from this network. Three full runs are summarised below; the tables are run 1.
- Scrutator wall time here is mostly timeouts waiting for Common Crawl, so it measures this
  network as much as the tool.

## Where Scrutator loses, and by how much

On breadth it loses clearly. On owasp.org, the one target with a real spread of names, the best
competitor (knockpy's recon engine) found 56 live names in 3 seconds; Scrutator found 2 in run 1
(crt.sh down), nothing in two runs (timeout), and 31 live names (51% of the 61-name union) only
after 36 minutes with a 3600 s allowance. sublist3r, a 2016 tool with one working source, also beats
Scrutator at the fixed timeout (24 live names in 12 s). On nmap.org Scrutator's recall is 0% of 4
(the truth is small and circular, but the names it missed, `scanme` among them, are exactly what a
person would look for). On example.com it ties at 100% of 1. On zonetransfer.me it is ahead of every
competitor, 4 names against 4 with only 2 live, but that is 13% of the zone, and 87% of the zone
is not reachable by any passive tool run here. Time: 150 to 330 s on small targets against 3 to 25 s
for knockpy, sublist3r and dnsrecon on those targets (dnsrecon needs 255 to 268 s on owasp.org),
and 2133 s for Scrutator on owasp.org. Precision is not a strength either: 4 of Scrutator's 5
names on example.com and 13 of 44 on owasp.org no longer resolve (they are historical certificate
names; the `ghost-hostname` risk flags them, but the hostname list does not separate them).
knockpy is worse on precision (0% and 42%), and its low-effort output is mostly junk
(349 of 357 nmap.org names are wildcard artifacts).

Where it wins: every reported hostname is backed by an evidence record that names it, on every scan
(competitors keep none); the signed bundle verifies against a pinned key and a one-byte change is
detected; it reports risks (2 to 6 per target: missing DMARC/CAA, SPF softfail, DNS concentration,
forgotten and ghost names) and a graph of relations, where the others print names; and its
report records which sources failed (commoncrawl, crt.sh, rdap), where the competitors return a
short list and at most a line on the console.

## The five biggest gaps, ranked

1. **Missing aggregator sources; the best competitor's output is mostly from them.** Per-source
   attribution on owasp.org (`bench/results/2026-10-17/attribution-owasp.org.json`): knockpy's
   hackertarget gave 50 names (49 live), rapiddns 23 (22 live), subdomain.center 122 (44 live);
   sublist3r's Netcraft gave 24 (all live); every other sublist3r engine gave 0. Scrutator has none
   of these (its sources are crt.sh, Wayback, Common Crawl, DNS, RDAP, ASN). Even in the 3600 s run
   it missed 25 of knockpy's 56 live names and 14 of Netcraft's 24. Planned for R1 (mnemonic pDNS,
   CertSpotter, urlscan); hackertarget, rapiddns, subdomain.center and Netcraft are not in that
   list and should be evaluated against their terms.
2. **crt.sh is a single point of failure for names.** crt.sh answered HTTP 502 to Scrutator in 4
   of the 11 scans that wrote a report. Where CT is the main source it decides the result: owasp.org
   gave 2 names when the seed query failed and 44 when it worked. No second CT source is consulted.
   dnsrecon (also crt.sh only) failed the same way on nmap.org in 2 of 3 runs. CertSpotter in R1
   is the fix; also retry the seed query separately from the per-name ones.
3. **Common Crawl timeouts consume the step budget and the clock, and a timeout leaves nothing.**
   In the 3600 s run, 45 of 45 Common Crawl calls failed with `ConnectTimeout` (3 attempts, 15 s
   connect each, plus backoff, about 47 s per call over 2133 s, and the module runs one call at a time), the scan stopped on
   "Step budget reached: some lookups were not run" after 60 module runs, and took 2133 s. At 900 s
   the scan was killed and wrote no report at all, although in an earlier, discarded harness run the database already
   held 46 names after the first two steps. Fixes: a per-source circuit breaker (stop calling a source that has failed N
   times in a row), run independent sources concurrently, and write a partial report on interrupt.
   Same cause explains 240 to 330 s on tiny targets.
4. **Stale names are reported as hostnames.** crt.sh and Wayback return every name ever seen;
   Scrutator keeps them all (4 of 5 on example.com, 13 of 44 on owasp.org no longer resolve), which
   is what pulls precision to 20% and 70% against sublist3r's 100%. The `ghost-hostname` risk flags
   them, but the report does not carry a live/stale state per hostname or sort by it. Resolving
   every discovered name once (it already resolves some) and recording the result as evidence fixes
   it without losing the history.
5. **The zone's own structure is out of reach, and no DNS-record technique fills the gap.** On
   zonetransfer.me all four tools together reach 4 of 31 names and Scrutator is the best of them,
   but 27 names (`vpn`, `owa`, `dc-office`, `asfdbbox`, `intns1`, the SRV and NAPTR owners) exist only
   in the zone. A zone transfer is active and would need an explicit, scope-gated module (planned
   R3+, "active modules gated by scope"); passive substitutes that Scrutator also lacks are NSEC/NSEC3
   walking where the zone allows it and following name-bearing record data (NS, MX, SRV, CNAME
   targets) of every known name as new in-scope candidates. This gap matters least for the
   criterion, since a passive competitor cannot close it either.

Also seen, smaller: `rdap returned HTTP 400` on 4 of 7 nmap.org lookups and 2 of 11 on owasp.org (run 1)
(a request shape Scrutator sends for subdomains), which shows up as a failed source without any
loss of names; and Scrutator has no wildcard-DNS detection, so on a wildcard zone it cannot tell
that three real certificate names are indistinguishable from any guess.

## Reproduce

```
python -m venv %USERPROFILE%\bench-venv
%USERPROFILE%\bench-venv\Scripts\python -m pip install dnsrecon sublist3r knock-subdomains
python bench/run_bench.py --date 2026-10-17          # about 25 minutes, Scrutator dominates
python bench/attribute_sources.py --date 2026-10-17  # per-source attribution on owasp.org
```

See `bench/README.md`. Expect different numbers: the public sources change and fail from run to
run, which is the point of the variance table.
